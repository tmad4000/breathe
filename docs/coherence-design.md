# Coherence prototype — design and validation

Keep respiration independent. Add contact fingertip PPG and experimental hands-free POS face PPG beside the existing chest motion tracker. All processing remains local. Use measured frame timestamps, reject missing/irregular frames, poor illumination and implausible beats; never fill a missing score with a previous value. Fingertip reports pulse intervals (not ECG RR), HR and RMSSD. Hands-free reports only a slow HR estimate and exploratory breath–heart coupling.

The 64-second RR window is interpolated at 4 Hz, detrended and Hann-windowed. Integrate spectral power from 0.0033–0.4 Hz; find the maximum in 0.04–0.26 Hz and integrate ±0.015 Hz. Ratio = peak/(total−peak). Labels are prototype thresholds, not HeartMath calibration. Reference: https://www.heartmath.org/research/science-of-the-heart/coherence/ . POS projection follows Wang et al., Algorithmic Principles of Remote PPG (2017). No claim of equivalence to HeartMath or clinical measurement.

UI retains the existing white surfaces, blue accents and system sans. Explicit mode selector, signal status, HR trace and blank warmup states. Resonance guide defaults to 6/min with 5.5/min available. Torch is best effort; bright external light is required when unavailable. Stop releases the camera and torch.

Validation before accuracy claims: simultaneous 5-minute seated recordings against an ECG chest strap with exported RR (preferred), plus Apple Watch HR as a coarse secondary comparison. Test multiple iPhones/Safari versions, skin tones, illumination, rear lenses, torch availability, hand pressure, motion, background/resume and thermal behavior. Compare beat matching, RR error, RMSSD bias and coherence agreement; quantify dropout separately. Hands-free requires synchronized respiration ground truth and phase/coupling comparison. Synthetic tests only establish algorithm behavior, not real-device accuracy. Stop if the camera/torch feels hot; breathe comfortably without forcing depth.

## v0.4.1 review corrections

The remote spectral trace uses beat-level HR, with HR and breath resampled onto the same ending timestamp. A synthetic POS signal modulated ±8 bpm at 0.1 Hz must retain >12 bpm peak-to-peak variation and score high. Failed analyses blank current output immediately while retaining history for recovery within <3 seconds; interpolation rejects gaps ≥3 seconds. This tolerance is for the slow HR/RR series, not permission to synthesize missing raw pulse cycles. Longer camera interruptions still require pulse reacquisition and a new valid coherence window. Pause/restart explicitly resets the session.

Hands-free shows breathing rate beside HR, independently of coherence availability. HR can appear after roughly 6–10 seconds of usable frames; the existing respiration estimator requires at least 15 seconds, and a trustworthy breathing rate may take longer, especially at 6/min (one cycle per 10 seconds). Do not relax respiration quality gates merely to show an early number.

## v0.4.2 timing and feedback review

Contact peaks are first located in the band-passed waveform, then refined with a local quadratic fitted to original source timestamps and green-channel samples. Exclude filter startup and right-edge peaks that lack sufficient support. This avoids deriving beat timing from a rounded resampled-array endpoint and reduces compressed-video amplitude quantization without smoothing consecutive RR intervals. Tests require constant-72-bpm RMSSD <10 ms in the actual clip browser pipeline and irregular-timestamp unit data, while retaining modulated-pulse variability. The fingertip ring displays HR until coherence is available, then its ratio; paced breathing still controls the arc. Warmup countdown uses the contiguous usable interval span, resets for gaps ≥3 seconds, and does not imply a guaranteed score from low-quality or flat data.
